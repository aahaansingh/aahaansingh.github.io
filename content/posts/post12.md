I do not find myself listening to much new music these days, at least by my old standards.
This is partly because I don't like playing music in the background while doing other things.
I also think that, as you accumulate a greater selection of music that you actually like, the risk/reward calculus of listening to new music changes.

Not only is it more enjoyable to listen to an old favorite when you're not feeling adventurous, but listening to an album for the second time is often as much an act of discovery as listening to one for the first.
That being said, I've encountered more music from this year that I've truly enjoyed than any other, and I thought that I'd share some of my favorites, songs and albums, in no particular order.
If I feel like it, I'll make another (because there's another post's worth), but these are the ones I've listened to enough to feel comfortable writing about.


## Mitski, "If I Leave" {#mitski-if-i-leave}

<iframe style="width:100%; aspect-ratio:16/9; height:auto;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/83yYI2bWN0U" title="Mitski - If I Leave (Official Video)" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This song has such wonderful structure to it, one that is constructed from the simplest of ingredients: four chords on loop.
But the way in which Mitski's voice slowly grows louder and more wretched, and the guitars distort and shift in rhythm, lifts the tension with pleasing linearity.
The lyrics follow the same narrative arc, all the way to that all-consuming climax; it's hard to come by such a nice correspondence.
They get at that self-pitying flavor of relationship doomsaying that I (and maybe you) relate to quite easily.
I find in them the pleasure and vulnerability of being recognized, which, on another level, is what the song is about in the first place.


## Sergeant, _Symbols_ {#sergeant-symbols}

<iframe style="width:100%; aspect-ratio:16/9; height:auto;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/zRJlivQz6X4" title="Shopping for an Avant-Garde Identity in the Bazaar of Life" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

All music is precedented, and good music is a matter of either perfecting a style or shifting it, and usually some combination of both.
Generally it is those perfections that are mellow on the ear, because they appeal to our convictions as to how music should sound, and the surprising music that grates and demands.
I'm not quite sure why _Symbols_, which is genuinely weird music, is so easy to listen to, but I suspect that this is because it grooves so pleasantly.
As long as you have solid and interesting rhythms, you can play anything on top and have it sound palatable at the very least.
More than being palatable, though, _Symbols_ is innovative and interesting music.
It is a strange and abstract evolution of dub (organic rather than electronic) that rambles through a lush forest of noise but never loses structure.
Curious, jaunty tales are scattered through the mist.
I am still trying to understand them.


## Murex, "Massacre" {#murex-massacre}

<iframe style="width:100%; aspect-ratio:16/9; height:auto;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/J9a52Y7QJkY" title="Murex - Massacre" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

Without a doubt, the best new song that I have heard this year.
I'm in awe of the fact that somebody out there could even concieve of such a work, let alone produce it.
The drums are doing crazy work (the thumping kicks, the scrambling hi-hats, the percussive echoes) but, just as a wall can be made of infinite varied arrangements of bricks, the aggregate effect is simple: that of rapid, immense motion.
Layers of simple melodies accrete through the song's progression, bubbling beneath a vocal performance that sounds raw because of the innumerable effects applied to it.
Despite this complexity, every single moment of every single track in this song makes perfect sense in relation to the other tracks and to the previous moment.
When the drums sputter and cut into a new section after a minute and a half, it seems to have been the most obvious way to break the escalating repetition of the previous section without really arresting the song's momentum, and it lends its ethereal character to the song's climax as well.
Again, I can't believe that there is so _much_ in here.


## Portraits of Tracy, _Q: From Where Do Our Primal Instincts Originate? A: MTV!_ {#portraits-of-tracy-q-from-where-do-our-primal-instincts-originate-a-mtv}

<iframe style="width:100%; aspect-ratio:16/9; height:auto;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/2pCH8bfzltA" title="Portraits Of Tracy - Caught In A Jam (Official Visualizer)" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This is sort of a weird EP for me because I'm generally not a fan of listening to "retro" stuff these days, even if the music I like skews older.
If I'm gonna listen to something that came out in the past decade, I want it to be something that I've never seen before.
I'm perfectly happy listening to that kind of music live, but on my own time, when I'm looking for something that touches my mind _and_ my heart?
Music that's trying to be retro generally doesn't cut it, because (if I can borrow from my earlier criteria) it's rarely as good as the original and, almost tautologically, isn't doing anything new.

But despite the '80s-driven "aesthetic" of this EP, I wouldn't classify it as a rehashing of the period.
In fact, none of it sounds like anything that came out in the '80s.
If anything, the music is heavily Bowie-inflected—from recalling his glam era in "Caught In A Jam" and "Royal Courts (Great Misfortune), to covering a soulful ballad for the final track, just as Bowie did on _Station to Station_.
I love Bowie, so that's a point in favor, but that doesn't address the retro question.
And indeed, save for "Halli Step / Raider Klan Shit," it isn't trying to sound
{{< sidenote "modern." >}}Except even Raider Klan is more than a decade old.{{< /sidenote >}}
But it takes a lot of different sounds, _old_ sounds, and combines them to somehow create something coherent.

Take "Caught In A Jam."
Again, that's a glam bop, but you've got a
{{< sidenote "Stereolab" >}}Even the Stereolab sound is retro, so we're creating recreations of recreations now.{{< /sidenote >}}
sort of organ sound where the piano would normally be (and a piano in the bridge, for good measure), heavily distorted drums, and singing that's more rhythmic than melodic.
That is to say that there isn't necessarily any innovation in the pieces used, but they are temporally dispersed and it takes something special to make a whole this compelling.
Part of it is taste, part of it is good production, and the rest is great songwriting.
The melodies are simple and catchy, while the story is amusingly delivered and tightly coupled to the structure of the song.
That last part is especially important because it's really difficult to tie together words and
{{< sidenote "sounds," >}}That's what I'm attempting to do right now!{{< /sidenote >}}
two completely different units of meaning, in a complementary way—of the songs I've talked about, only "If I Leave" really does it, and that's with a far simpler story and structure.
This song is labyrinthine.


## $quib, "Ephus roundshot &amp; floater" {#quib-ephus-roundshot-and-floater}

<iframe style="width:100%; aspect-ratio:16/9; height:auto;" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/FKPmfCHyWC0" title="$quib - Ephus roundshot &amp; floater" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This one's made by a guy from Oberlin, Brock Bierly and his fictional cyclops friend, which is the sort of bit that's embarassing if you're bad at making music and weird if you're good at it.
That describes this style of music as well, because when you're this vulnerable in a song, there's a great risk of falling into self pitying navel-gazing.
I'm reminded of Christgau's review of _Pornography_:
>I mean, why so glum, chum? Cheer up; look on the bright side. You got your contract, right? And your synthesizers, bet you'll have fun with them. Believe me, kid, it will pass.

I thought that this was a very stupid review when I first read it but Christgau is describing this same risk of failing to speak to the listener and thus appearing inauthentic, no matter how authentic the artist's emotions really
{{< sidenote "are." >}}And Christgau's entire system seems to revolve around his notion of authenticity.{{< /sidenote >}}
It's one of my favorite albums, but I find myself agreeing with Christgau more and more on _Pornography_ as I become a happier person because it's such an extravagant and absurd display of emotion, well beyond anything I could conceptualize anybody as really having felt.
On the other end is music that is too timid, which is both more consumable and less interesting because it necessarily lacks content.

This song is not silly, though, if you're willing to put a little effort into suspending your ridiculometer.
The number of ways in which Bierly ties those words, "ephus," "roundshot," and "floater," to the narrative of a strained relationship (situationship?) is evocative and resonant.
There's also a very off-kilter, terminally online quality to the music, with all of the vocal chops and trap hi-hats and electronic noise.
It's melodic enough to catch my ear and harsh enough to hold it; in that sense, it reminds me of Portishead's "Machine Gun."
Yeah, I guess listening to this is like being shot in the chest by a machine gun, or being splayed on a cannonball for miles and days, or getting struck out by an ephus.
